Personal guarantees

A limited company protects you, until you sign a personal guarantee. Here’s what that means, and what to do if one gets called in.

5 minute read. Covers England and Wales.

The short version

  1. A guarantee lets the lender come after you personally, not just the company.
  2. It usually only gets called in if the company defaults or goes under.
  3. So a plan that keeps the company paying is often your best protection.

What it is

Your promise to pay a company debt if the company can’t. It sits outside the protection a limited company gives you, so the lender can come after your own money, and in the worst case, your home.

“Joint and several”

If more than one director signed, the lender can usually chase any one of you for the full amount, not just your share.

Where they turn up

  • Business loans

    Most lenders ask for one.

  • Cash advances

    Many include one. Check what yours covers.

  • Asset finance

    Common for newer companies.

  • Leases

    Landlords often want the rent guaranteed.

  • Supplier accounts

    Sometimes hidden in the small print.

Covid loans are different

Check which scheme your loan was under.

Bounce Back Loans

No guarantee doesn’t mean no risk. If a Bounce Back Loan was misused, directors can still be made personally responsible.

How a guarantee gets called in

  1. 1

    Company defaults

    Or goes into insolvency.

  2. 2

    Formal demand

    The lender writes to you personally.

  3. 3

    Court claim

    If the demand isn’t paid.

  4. 4

    Bankruptcy risk

    For debts of £5,000 or more.

Keep the company paying, and it usually never gets past step 1.

Check your guarantee

Tick what you know. Anything you can’t answer, ask the lender for a copy of what you signed.

If yours has been called in

  1. Don’t ignore it

    Silence makes court action more likely.

  2. Get the paperwork

    The guarantee, the loan agreement and the demand.

  3. Get legal advice

    On whether it can be enforced, and for how much.

  4. Talk to the lender

    Many will agree a plan or a settlement.

For advice on your own finances, speak to a solicitor or get free debt advice from National Debtline.

Other ways directors become liable

Even without a guarantee.

Overdrawn director’s loan

If the company goes into liquidation, you can be asked to pay it back.

Wrongful trading

Carrying on when you knew insolvency couldn’t be avoided, without protecting creditors.

Misconduct

Courts can order disqualified directors to repay creditors’ losses. Bans can last up to 15 years.

Some unpaid taxes

HMRC can make directors personally liable in some cases, like National Insurance unpaid through fraud or neglect.

Before you sign another one

  • Ask for a cap on what you can owe
  • Keep your home out of it
  • Ask for it to end once the balance drops
  • Get independent legal advice first
  • Look at personal guarantee insurance for new guarantees
