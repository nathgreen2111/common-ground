Statutory demands and winding-up petitions

How a creditor can force a company into liquidation, how long each stage takes, and what you can do at each one.

5 minute read. Covers England and Wales.

How it usually plays out

  1. Day 0

    Statutory demand

    A creditor owed £750 or more serves a formal demand on the company.

  2. Day 21

    Deadline

    The company has 21 days to pay or agree terms.

  3. Petition

    Petition issued

    The creditor asks the court to wind the company up.

  4. +7 days

    Advertised

    At least 7 business days after it’s served, it appears in the Gazette. Banks usually freeze the account.

  5. Hearing

    Winding-up order

    The court can put the company into liquidation.

A creditor doesn’t always need a statutory demand first. An unpaid court judgment, for example, can be enough to go straight to a petition.

  1. When a demand arrives
  2. Once a petition is issued

When a demand arrives

  • Check it’s genuine and the amount is right
  • Don’t ignore it. The 21 days start from when it’s served.
  • Pay it, or contact the creditor inside the 21 days with a realistic offer
  • If you genuinely dispute the debt, get legal advice quickly. The court can stop a petition over a real dispute.

This is the best moment to get help. Before a petition, creditors have every reason to accept a sensible plan.

Once a petition is issued

Move fast, and aim to deal with it before it’s advertised. That’s the point where the bank usually freezes the account.

Paying the debt and the creditor’s costs before the hearing usually ends it. Other creditors can join the petition, though, so one payment doesn’t always close it. A licensed insolvency practitioner can explain formal options, some of which can stop a petition.

If the bank account is frozen

The company can ask the court for a “validation order” to keep trading, but it’s costly and needs strong evidence. Getting ahead of the advert is far easier.

If HMRC is the creditor

HMRC issues a lot of winding-up petitions. Contact them about a Time to Pay arrangement as early as you can. It’s much easier to agree before a petition than after.
