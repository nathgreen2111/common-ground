Statutory demands and winding-up petitions

How a creditor can force a company into liquidation, and what you can do at each stage.

4 minute read. Covers England and Wales.

21days to act on a statutory demand

The short version

  1. A creditor owed £750 or more can start the process.
  2. Once a demand is served, you have 21 days to act.
  3. Once a petition is advertised, the bank usually freezes the account.

How it plays out

  1. Day 0

    Demand

    A formal demand is served on the company.

  2. Day 21

    Deadline

    Pay or agree terms by now.

  3. Petition

    Petition

    The creditor asks the court to wind you up.

  4. +7 days

    Advertised

    It’s published. The bank freezes the account.

  5. Hearing

    Liquidation

    The court can wind the company up.

A creditor can sometimes skip the demand, for example if a court judgment has gone unpaid.

What to do at each stage

Demand

When a demand arrives

  • Check the amount is right
  • Pay, or make a realistic offer inside 21 days
  • Disputed debt? Get legal advice fast
Petition

Once a petition is issued

  • Act before it’s advertised
  • Paying the debt and costs usually ends it
  • Speak to a licensed insolvency practitioner
Frozen

If the account is frozen

  • The court can grant a “validation order”
  • It’s costly and needs strong evidence
  • Far easier to act before the advert

The best time to get help is before a petition. That’s when creditors have every reason to accept a sensible plan.

If HMRC is the creditor

HMRC issues a lot of winding-up petitions. Ask about a Time to Pay arrangement as early as you can. It’s much easier to agree before a petition than after.
